We are asked to find how many ways **5 people** can sit around a **round table**, given that **two of them refuse to sit next to each other**.

---

### Step 1: Total number of ways to seat 5 people around a round table

When arranging people **around a round table**, the number of distinct arrangements is:

$$
(n - 1)! = (5 - 1)! = 4! = 24
$$

This is because in circular permutations, one position is fixed to account for rotational symmetry.

---

### Step 2: Subtract the number of arrangements where the two people **do sit next to each other**

Let’s call the two people who refuse to sit next to each other **A** and **B**.

We want to count the number of arrangements where **A and B are sitting next to each other**, and then subtract that from the total.

#### Step 2.1: Treat A and B as a single "block"

If A and B are sitting together, we can treat them as a single unit or "block". So instead of 5 people, we now have:

- 1 block (A-B or B-A)
- 3 other people

So we have **4 units** to arrange around the table.

Number of circular arrangements of 4 units:

$$
(4 - 1)! = 3! = 6
$$

#### Step 2.2: Within the block, A and B can be arranged in 2 ways: A-B or B-A

So total number of arrangements where A and B are sitting together:

$$
3! \times 2 = 6 \times 2 = 12
$$

---

### Step 3: Subtract from total

Total number of arrangements where A and B **do not sit next to each other**:

$$
\text{Total} - \text{Arrangements with A and B together} = 24 - 12 = 12
$$

---

### ✅ Final Answer:

$$
\boxed{12}
$$

There are **12** ways to seat 5 people around a round table such that two of them do **not** sit next to each other.
