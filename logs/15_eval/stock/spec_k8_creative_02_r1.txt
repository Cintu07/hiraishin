The city stirs in the hush of rain,  
its breath a murmur through the glass,  
each window a lantern,  
each street a river of light.  

The sky weeps, but the city does not cry—  
it hums, a low, steady tune,  
as if the rain is just another kind of song,  
a rhythm the city has always known.  

Buses groan through the mist,  
their wheels slipping on the wet,  
and the world is quiet,  
except for the sound of water  
falling, falling, falling—  
a thousand drops, a thousand voices,  
all whispering the same thing:  
*Wake up. Wake up. Wake up.*

The coffee shops open their doors,  
steam rising like ghosts,  
and the smell of bread and beans  
mixes with the scent of damp stone.  

Children run through the puddles,  
their laughter sharp and bright,  
a contrast to the gray,  
a spark in the dark.  

The city is not asleep—  
it is alive,  
wet and breathing,  
its heart beating in the rain.
