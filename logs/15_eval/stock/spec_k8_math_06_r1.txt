We are given the function:

$$
f(x) = x^2 \cdot \sin(x)
$$

---

### **Step 1: Find the derivative $ f'(x) $**

We use the **product rule**, which states:

$$
(fg)' = f'g + fg'
$$

Let:
- $ f(x) = x^2 $
- $ g(x) = \sin(x) $

Then:
- $ f'(x) = 2x $
- $ g'(x) = \cos(x) $

Apply the product rule:

$$
f'(x) = \frac{d}{dx}(x^2 \cdot \sin(x)) = 2x \cdot \sin(x) + x^2 \cdot \cos(x)
$$

So the derivative is:

$$
f'(x) = 2x \sin(x) + x^2 \cos(x)
$$

---

### **Step 2: Find the equation of the tangent line at $ x = 0 $**

To find the equation of the tangent line at a point, we need:

- The value of the function at $ x = 0 $: $ f(0) $
- The derivative at $ x = 0 $: $ f'(0) $

#### **Evaluate $ f(0) $:**

$$
f(0) = 0^2 \cdot \sin(0) = 0
$$

#### **Evaluate $ f'(0) $:**

$$
f'(0) = 2(0)\sin(0) + (0)^2 \cos(0) = 0 + 0 = 0
$$

So the slope of the tangent line at $ x = 0 $ is 0, and the point is $ (0, 0) $.

#### **Equation of the tangent line:**

Using the point-slope form:

$$
y - y_1 = m(x - x_1)
$$

Where:
- $ (x_1, y_1) = (0, 0) $
- $ m = 0 $

So the equation is:

$$
y - 0 = 0(x - 0) \Rightarrow y = 0
$$
