The lighthouse on the edge of the world had stood for over a century, its beam cutting through the dark like a promise. Elias had been its keeper for ten years, ever since the old man who'd raised him passed away. The sea was his only companion, and the silence of the cliffs was his home.

One stormy evening, as the wind howled like a wounded beast, Elias was cleaning the lens when he noticed something unusual. A bottle, half-buried in the sand near the base of the tower, had been washed up by the tide. Curious, he picked it up, its glass cool and smooth, and uncorked it with a trembling hand.

Inside was a single sheet of paper, the ink faded but legible. The message was written in a careful, deliberate hand:

*"To the Keeper of the Light,  
If you are reading this, I am lost. I was shipwrecked off the coast of the Isle of Skye, and I have no idea how to find my way home. I have no family, no friends, only this bottle and the hope that someone, somewhere, will find it. If you can, please send help. I am not afraid of the sea, but I am afraid of being forgotten."*

Elias read the note twice, his heart pounding. He had never imagined such a thing—someone writing to him, as if he were a savior, a beacon in the dark. He sat on the steps of the tower, the note clutched in his hands, and let the wind carry his thoughts away.

The next morning, he set out with the bottle and the note, navigating the rocky shore with care. He found the wrecked ship, its hull half-submerged, and the figure of a man, barely conscious, clinging to the wreckage. Elias called for help, and by dusk, a rescue boat arrived.

The man, a sailor named Finn, was grateful beyond words. He had been adrift for weeks, surviving on scraps and hope. When he learned that the message had been found and that he was safe, he wept.

Elias returned to the lighthouse that night, the bottle empty, the note now a memory. He had never been a hero, but in that moment, he had been something more. And the light, he realized, was not just for the sea—it was for those who needed to be found.
