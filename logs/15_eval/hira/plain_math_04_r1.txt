To find the **average speed** for the entire trip, we need to use the formula for **average speed**, which is:

$$
\text{Average speed} = \frac{\text{Total distance}}{\text{Total time}}
$$

---

### Step 1: Calculate the total distance

The train travels **180 km** to a destination and then **180 km** back, so:

$$
\text{Total distance} = 180 + 180 = 360 \text{ km}
$$

---

### Step 2: Calculate the time taken for each leg of the trip

- **Outbound trip (180 km at 60 km/h):**

$$
\text{Time} = \frac{\text{Distance}}{\text{Speed}} = \frac{180}{60} = 3 \text{ hours}
$$

- **Return trip (180 km at 90 km/h):**

$$
\text{Time} = \frac{180}{90} = 2 \text{ hours}
$$

---

### Step 3: Calculate total time

$$
\text{Total time} = 3 + 2 = 5 \text{ hours}
$$

---

### Step 4: Calculate average speed

$$
\text{Average speed} = \frac{360}{5} = 72 \text{ km/h}
$$

---

### Why is the average speed **not 75 km/h**?

You might think to just average the two speeds:

$$
\frac{60 + 90}{2} = 75 \text{ km/h}
$$

But this is **not** the correct way to calculate average speed when the **distances are the same** but the **speeds are different**.

The **average speed** is not the arithmetic mean of the two speeds, because the time spent at each speed is different. The train spends more time traveling at the slower speed (60 km/h), so the average speed is **weighted** more toward the slower speed.

---

### Final Answer:

$$
\boxed{72 \text{ km/h}}
$$

The average speed is **not 75 km/h** because average speed is calculated based on **total distance divided by total time**, not the simple average of the two speeds.
