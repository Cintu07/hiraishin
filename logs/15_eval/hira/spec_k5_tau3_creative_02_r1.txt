The city stirs in the hush of rain,  
its breath a whisper through the glass,  
each window a mirror to the sky,  
reflecting the gray of morning's sigh.

Streetlamps flicker like distant stars,  
their light a fragile, fleeting grace,  
while the pavement drinks the sky,  
and the world is washed in quiet space.

A bus rumbles through the mist,  
its wheels a rhythm on the wet,  
and the trees, still, stretch their limbs  
to touch the clouds that hang so thick.

The coffee shops exhale steam,  
a ghost of warmth in the chill,  
and the sidewalks hum with the sound  
of boots and wheels and whispered will.

A child runs, a flag of joy,  
through the rain that paints her dress,  
her laughter lost in the wind,  
a song the city can’t resist.

The rain is not a curse, but a hymn,  
a lullaby for the waking town,  
where every drop is a promise,  
and every street is a new sound.

The city breathes, and the world is new,  
in the arms of the morning rain,  
where the silence speaks in silver,  
and the sky begins to change its name.
