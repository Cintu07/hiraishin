The lighthouse on the edge of the world had stood for over a century, its beam cutting through the dark like a promise. Elias had been its keeper for ten years, ever since the old man who'd raised him passed away. The sea was his only companion, and the silence of the cliffs was his home.

One stormy evening, as the wind howled like a wounded beast, Elias was cleaning the lens when he noticed something unusual. A bottle, half-buried in the sand near the base of the tower, had been washed up by the tide. Curious, he pried it free and uncorked it with a trembling hand.

Inside was a single sheet of paper, the ink faded but legible. The message was written in a careful, deliberate hand:

*"To the Keeper of the Light,  
If you are reading this, I am lost. I was shipwrecked off the coast of the Isle of Skye, and I have no idea how to reach the mainland. I have no food, no water, and no hope. If you can hear me, please help me. I am not alone in this. I am waiting for the light to guide me home."*

Elias read the note again, his heart pounding. He had never seen the Isle of Skye from this vantage point, but he knew the waters well. The next morning, he set out with a small boat, the bottle clutched in his hand like a prayer.

He found the wrecked vessel, half-submerged and battered by the sea. Inside, he found a young man, barely conscious, his face pale and drawn. Elias carried him back to the lighthouse, where he tended to his wounds and fed him with whatever he had.

Days passed, and the young man, named Finn, recovered. He spoke of his journey, of the storm that had taken his ship, and of the hope that the lighthouse had given him. As the days turned to weeks, Finn stayed, helping Elias with the chores, and the two became unlikely friends.

One evening, as the light swept the horizon, Finn looked up and said, "You saved me, Elias. You gave me a light to follow."

Elias smiled. "And you gave me a reason to keep shining."
